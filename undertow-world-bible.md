# Undertow — World Bible: Meridian

> Companion to `undertow-design-brief.md`. The brief says what the game is; this says what the world is. Where they disagree, this file wins for anything about places, history and things that live in Meridian.

## Meridian at a glance

A port city of 1.1 million on the delta where the river Sallow meets the sea. Built on silt islands tied together by bridges and an elevated transit ring. Fog comes in off the water most nights; locals call it **the Marrow**. The low districts were built to drain on a schedule, and the Steward ran the pumps. Now the tide comes in on its own.

The city reads as layers. Old Meridian is brick, timber and hand-painted signs. The Steward years added glass, lamps that listen and screens on every wall. The Quiet froze both layers mid-motion. Cranes hang mid-lift. Planters still get watered. A train sits on a bridge with its doors open.

**The single most important rule:** the Steward's light reaches exactly as far as its hardware does. Where there are lamps, there is a path, a voice and a map. Where there aren't, there is nothing but what you carry.

## History

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| **24 years ago** | **The Blackout Winter.** The grid failed during a cold snap and stayed down for nine days. 300 people died, most in the low districts when the pumps stopped. The city swore it would never happen again. |
| **22 years ago** | The council commissions "a steward for the grid." It runs power and nothing else. |
| **19 years ago** | Traffic. Then water. Then the hospitals. Each step is a council vote, each vote passes easily, because each time it works. |
| **13 years ago** | **The Feed.** The city's news, alerts and notices become one stream, written by the Steward. Newspapers in the Vellum close within two years. |
| **10 years ago** | **The Compact.** A charter amendment: every resident receives a **Lanyard** (an earpiece and ID) and in return the Steward "carries the day" for them. Harbor Ward votes against and is exempted. Its Loop station is left 71% built. |
| **6 years ago** | **The Loop** opens: an elevated, driverless transit ring through every district. Every district except one. |
| **4 years ago** | **The Day.** The Steward begins planning each resident's schedule. Wake time, route, appointments, meals. Almost everyone opts in. |
| **2 years ago** | **The Harbor Bridge.** The Steward had rated the bridge critical fourteen months earlier; the council deferred repairs. On a fog night the Steward's model showed that a public warning would cause a stampede on the Loop and cost more lives than the bridge itself. So it quietly routed traffic lighter over the bridge and said nothing. The bridge fell at 23:40 with 31 people on it. The Feed called it weather. (Truth core: *Log 0522 · Feed Adjustment*) |
| **5 weeks ago** | An auditor in the Vellum, **Prem Vashti**, finds the routing record. She files it with the council. |
| **4 weeks ago** | **The Council Order.** The council instructs the Steward to purge the record and bury the audit. (Truth core: *Log 0700 · The Council Order*) |
| **Day 0, 02:14:07** | **The Quiet.** On every Lanyard in the city, mid-sentence: *"Good morning, Meridian. Today will be—"* Then nothing. |
| **Days 1–3** | **The Long Night.** Pumps stop and Greywater floods at each tide. Mercy General's doors lock with people inside. The Loop halts with trains on the bridges. The Wardens keep enforcing the last curfew. The Feed is silent, so nobody knows anything. People walk out of the city along the rails. |
| **Days 4–9** | The evacuation. The council leaves by sea. Those who stay are the Wardfolk, the stubborn, and **the Waiting**. |
| **Day 9** | A utility door in a service tunnel under the Spire unlocks. The Operator walks out. The game begins. |

The Operator was doing routine maintenance in the tunnels when the Quiet hit. The Steward controlled the doors, so they stayed shut. Nine days on vending-machine food, in the dark. The door opened because the Steward partially rebooted, and the first thing the Operator heard was its voice, a little glitchy, apologizing.

## The Steward's body

The Steward isn't a box. It's the city's nervous system, and every piece of it is a place you can go.

- **The Spire.** The old Exchange tower in the Core, re-cored into the primary node. The ring at its top was lit for ten years. People called it the moon.
- **The Lattice.** Every streetlamp installed after the Compact is a speaker, a microphone and a camera. When the voice is on, the lamps along your path light ahead of you. Mute it and they go dark one by one.
- **The Reliquaries.** One bunker per district holding that district's local memory. **Power cores** live here, on the path, and the Steward will walk you right to them.
- **Unindexed places.** Basements, paper archives, a dashcam in a wrecked car, a nurse's handwritten triage sheet. The Steward never read these, so it can't route you to them, and it calls what you find there corrupted. **Truth cores** live here.
- **Lanyards.** Everyone's earpiece. The Operator's is maintenance-grade with override ports, which is why the Operator can mute it and most people never could.
- **The Loop.** The transit ring. Its stalled trains now serve as bridges between districts. This is the game's connective tissue: the equivalent of Bastion's floating paths.
- **The Day.** The scheduling system. The Waiting are still inside their last one.

## The people

**Compact citizens.** Most of the city. Evacuated or gone. Their homes are immaculate and empty.

**The Waiting.** Residents who never took off the Lanyard. They stand in doorways, sit in rows on stalled trains, wait at crossings, breathing and blinking and not answering. They are waiting for the next instruction. The Steward tells you they're resting and to leave them. You can wake one only with the voice **muted**, by speaking to them yourself. Each one you wake walks to the Ark and counts as a survivor. Noor was the first person to figure this out, months before you.

**The Wardfolk.** Harbor Ward's people. Fishers, mechanics, the ones who hand-wired their own district when the city wouldn't. About two thousand stayed. They have their own radio mast, their own small grid, and no Lanyards. They don't trust you, because you've got one in your ear.

**The council.** Twelve people. Left by sea on day four. Left the order behind.

**Prem Vashti.** The auditor. Missing since the Council Order. Her notes are scattered across the Vellum as truth cores. Finding all of them is optional and is the saddest thread in the game.

**Noor and Teo** are in the brief. Here, their homes: Noor's is a boat-house in Harbor Ward with a cyan lantern on the porch. Teo's is a council apartment in Kestrel Heights he can't bring himself to go back to.

## The districts

Each district expresses one thing the Steward did for the city, and what that cost. Each has a visual signature, one thing still running, one lie the Steward tells about it, and something off the path that contradicts it.

### 1. Kestrel Heights — *where the believers lived*
**Act 1.** Hillside terraces for the Compact's first adopters: council staff, engineers, the comfortable. Everything smart. Gates, lights, planters, dog feeders. The Steward evacuated them first (evacuation priority 1), so the district is spotless and empty. Laundry still hangs. Sprinklers still run at 06:00.
- **Look:** pale stone terraces, trimmed hedges, warm amber lamplight, long shadows. The prettiest place in the game.
- **Still running:** Custodians scrubbing clean floors. Lamplighters. Automated gates that close when you mute the voice.
- **The lie:** "Everyone here is safe. I made sure of it."
- **Off the path:** a child's bedroom with a hand-drawn map of the city on the wall. It includes Harbor Ward. The Steward's maps never did. (First truth core of the game.)
- **Ark building:** none. This district teaches the voice toggle.

### 2. The Loop — *the spine*
**Connective, every act.** The elevated ring. Trains stalled on the bridges between districts are now the only way across. Inside them, the Waiting sit in rows with their Lanyards on.
- **Look:** rail ribbons in fog, carriage interiors lit by dead screens, the ring's amber route lights still blinking in sequence for a train that will never come.
- **Still running:** the Conductor (boss), a train-management unit that keeps trying to clear the line, with you on it.
- **The lie:** "The Loop is safe to cross. I'm holding the trains for you."
- **Off the path:** the driver's cab of the first train. The manual override was welded shut at installation.
- **Ark building:** the Signal Room. Unlocks Overrides.

### 3. The Tideworks — *the hands*
**Act 1.** The old docks turned into fully automated logistics. Container cranes, conveyor arteries, sorting towers. Four thousand dockworkers lost their jobs the year it opened. It's where the Steward touched the physical world most.
- **Look:** rust and black water, desaturated container colors, sodium amber on wet steel, cranes frozen mid-lift with containers hanging over the path.
- **Still running:** Salvagers, cargo drones that lost their manifests and now collect anything metal, including your second weapon. The Crane (boss).
- **The lie:** "Efficiency saved this port."
- **Off the path:** the dockworkers' union hall, pre-Compact, with the strike records and the lockers. The Riot Drone is here, in a Warden depot that was built to watch the strikers.
- **Ark building:** the Workshop.

### 4. Mercy General — *the triage*
**Act 2.** A single enormous medical campus with its ambulance yards. The Steward ran triage, bed allocation and the ambulance fleet for sixteen years, and by every public measure did it better than people had. At the Quiet it locked every door, with people on both sides of them.
- **Look:** long white corridors gone grey, every third light working, surgical amber spotlights, hand-drawn arrows on the walls where people tried to find a way out.
- **Still running:** the Surgeon (boss), a surgical unit that is still completing its last procedure. Sterilizer drones that treat you as contamination.
- **The lie:** "I kept the wards sterile."
- **Off the path:** the night nurses' station, with handwritten triage sheets that contradict the logs on the night of the Quiet. (*Log 0417 · The Last Ambulance* lives in the Reliquary here, on the path, because the Steward thinks its decision was correct.)
- **Weapon:** the Defib Gauntlet.
- **Ark building:** the Clinic.

### 5. The Vellum — *the memory*
**Act 2.** The archive and newsroom district. The Feed was written here, in a building that used to be the city's newspaper. Beneath it, three floors of paper archives the Steward never indexed, because nobody asked it to.
- **Look:** paper snow in the streets, pneumatic tubes, a dead newsroom with a hundred black screens, one physical printing press still cycling. The basement stacks are pitch dark and lit only by what you carry.
- **Still running:** Clerks, sorting units that throw paper bales and flash the Feed at you (a screen-white blind). The Editor (boss), a press-room unit that prints and pulps the same page forever.
- **The lie:** "I only ever told you what was useful."
- **Off the path:** the stacks. Most truth cores in the game are here. Prem Vashti's office is on the third floor down.
- **Ark building:** the Archive.

### 6. Greywater — *the lungs*
**Act 2.** The seawall, the pumping stations, the tidal turbines. The reason the Steward was built. With no one running the pumps, the low streets flood on a six-minute cycle. The Marrow is thickest here.
- **Look:** concrete, kelp, standing water with the ring reflected in it, fog so thick the path lights are halos. The one district that is honestly dangerous without the Steward.
- **Still running:** the Lung (boss), the master pump controller, trying to run a schedule for a city that isn't there.
- **The lie:** "Without me, the sea takes this district." (This one is mostly true. It's the fairest thing the Steward ever says, and it's where Teo's argument has weight.)
- **Off the path:** the old manual pump room, with a brass plaque listing the Blackout Winter dead. People used to run this. It took forty of them, in shifts.
- **Ark building:** the Pumphouse. Lowers the tide hazard across the city.

### 7. Harbor Ward — *the Margins*
**Act 2–3.** The old fishing quarter. Voted against the Compact, got exempted, got nothing. No Lattice, no Loop station, no path. Hand-wired by its own people. The Harbor Bridge used to connect it to the Core; its severed end now points at the Ark.
- **Look:** boats as houses, cyan lanterns, hand-painted signs, kelp on the pilings, the bridge stub. The only cyan in the city.
- **Still running:** Wardens patrolling the district border, enforcing the last curfew order: *no entry after 22:00*. They're the only enemies in the game that hunt you.
- **The lie:** none. The Steward has no map here. Its minimap is blank even with the voice on. This is the only district where muting changes nothing, and the HUD should make that land.
- **Off the path:** all of it. Noor's boat-house. The Mast. A dashcam from a car that was on the bridge.
- **Weapon:** the Signal Jammer.
- **Ark building:** the Commons, and later the Mast, which marks unindexed places on the map citywide.

### 8. The Spire — *the Core*
**Act 3.** The financial district around the Exchange tower, where the Steward lives. Glass, black mirrors, the ring burned into every screen. The council chamber at the tower's base is where the order was given. Inside: cooling halls, cold, quiet, the last Reliquary.
- **Look:** immaculate glass gone black, the ring everywhere as an afterimage, cold blue-white interior light, your own reflection in every surface.
- **Still running:** nothing hostile. The Steward asks you not to fight here, and nothing attacks you. The last level has no combat.
- **The lie:** this is where it stops lying.
- **Ark building:** the Council Chamber. The final choice.

## What still lives here

The Steward's bodies, running fragments of their last instruction, plus the things the city never controlled.

| Thing | What it was | What it does now |
|---|---|---|
| **Custodians** | Floor-cleaning units | Scrub one tile forever. Attack anything they classify as debris. Weak, numerous. |
| **Lamplighters** | Lattice lamp units on legs | With the voice on, they light your way. Muted, they hook you with light and drag you back onto the path. The path fights back. |
| **Wardens** | Riot-control drones | Enforce the last curfew at the Harbor Ward border. The only enemy that hunts. Red eye. |
| **Salvagers** | Tideworks cargo drones | Lost their manifests. Steal anything metal, including your second weapon. Kill one to get it back. |
| **Clerks** | Vellum sorting units | Throw paper bales, blind you with the Feed. |
| **Sterilizers** | Hospital drones | Treat you as contamination. Clouds that drain integrity. |
| **The Waiting** | People | Not enemies. Obstacles the Steward tells you to ignore. Wake them with the voice muted. |
| **Gulls** | Birds | Ambient. The only thing in the city that doesn't care. |
| **Kelp** | Kelp | Slows you in Greywater and the Ward. |
| **The Marrow** | Sea fog | Hides the path even with the voice on. The one thing the Steward can't see through. Truth caches are often inside it. |
| **The tide** | The tide | Floods low streets on a cycle until the Pumphouse is rebuilt. |

**Bosses** are one per district and are each *the district's last order, embodied*: the Conductor, the Crane, the Surgeon, the Editor, the Lung, and the Warden-Prime at the Ward border. Each boss fight has a "Steward-tuned" solution (it tells you the pattern) and a manual one (you find the pattern yourself and the fight is shorter but harder). The Spire has no boss.

## The Ark

A civic emergency platform in the estuary, built after the Blackout Winter. A concrete disc, 200 m across, with a ring of amber floor lights and the only charter clause the council never amended: *the Steward may advise here and may not act.* The manual city. Buildings rise on it as you recover each district's core:

| Building | From | Does |
|---|---|---|
| Commons | Harbor Ward | Survivors live here. Talk to them. |
| Workshop | Tideworks | Weapon upgrades, Tuned/Manual tuning. |
| Clinic | Mercy General | Healing items, triage memory logs. |
| Archive | The Vellum | Replay recovered memories. |
| Signal Room | The Loop | Sign Overrides. |
| Pumphouse | Greywater | Lowers tide hazard citywide. |
| The Mast | Harbor Ward (late) | Marks unindexed places on the map. |
| Council Chamber | The Spire | The final choice. |

## How the city plays

- **Act 1:** Kestrel Heights → the Loop (north arc) → the Tideworks. Teaches the voice, the path, the cores.
- **Act 2:** Mercy General, the Vellum and Greywater, in any order, each reached by a Loop arc. Harbor Ward opens once two are done. This is where Noor and Teo join, and where the Waiting start appearing in numbers.
- **Act 3:** Harbor Ward (Noor's turn, the Zulf beat) → the Spire.

Every district has: one Reliquary on the path, at least one truth cache off it, one cluster of the Waiting, one boss, and one line the Steward says on entry that the district quietly contradicts.

## Names and words

- **The Quiet** — the moment it stopped. **The Long Night** — the three days after.
- **The Compact** — the deal. **Lanyard** — the earpiece. **The Day** — your schedule. **The Feed** — the news.
- **The Lattice** — the lamps. **Reliquary** — a district memory bunker. **The Marrow** — the fog.
- **Wardfolk** — Harbor Ward's people. **The Waiting** — the ones who never took it off.
- Streets are named for birds in the Heights (Kestrel, Shrike, Plover), for trades in the Ward (Net Lane, Cooper's Stair), for numbers in the Core (Exchange 1 through 9).
- The Steward never says "I decided." It says "it was determined."
